# Purpose

- Summary of the plan to complete and publish the GD-CBD (glucose dendrimer-cannabidiol) postoperative pain work.
- For review by collaborators and PIs.
- **Main ask:** sign-off on a combined pain-plus-epilepsy paper (see "What we need," at the end).

# Strategy: two paths, one decision point

- **Default:** a combined paper, covering both the epilepsy data and the postoperative pain data.
- **Shared finding the combined paper is built on:** GD-CBD reduces inflammatory markers in both models, measured with the same cytokine panel (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, IL-10) in both species.
- **Fallback:** a pain-only short report, more conservative claims, if the combined paper isn't approved or the new pain cohort doesn't replicate.
- **Decision point:** 2026-11-13, once the new pain cohort is unblinded.

| If the new pain cohort... | Then the paper is... |
|:---|:---|
| Replicates the earlier effect, or is at least consistent with it | Combined pain-plus-epilepsy paper. Pain is one supporting figure. |
| Does not replicate | Epilepsy-led paper, with chemistry and in vitro data. No pain efficacy claim. |
| Either result, if the combined paper isn't approved | Pain-only short report, conservative claims. |

# Where the pain data currently stand

| Fact | What it means for the paper |
|:---|:---|
| Two original cohorts showed a clear, sustained GD-CBD effect over 5+ days, both from the same original CBD source material. | This is the finding the paper rests on. |
| Several later cohorts, made with different material or methods, did not reproduce the effect. One, at a much higher dose, showed a partial effect. | Reported transparently in a supplement table listing every cohort and preparation, not glossed over. |
| Standard chemical characterization (purity, size, NMR) has not predicted which batches work in vivo. Two failed batches looked clean by every standard measure. | The best current quality check is functional, not structural: whether the material reduces TNF-α release in an inflammatory cell assay. |
| The new cohort now in preparation uses a new CBD source lot from the original supplier. | This is the critical test. If it reproduces the effect, it's a genuine replication with independent material, not just two cohorts sharing early material. |

The claim in the paper rests on what replicates, not on the full historical record.

# Remaining experimental work

| Area | What's being done |
|:---|:---|
| **Chemistry, in vitro** (must finish before the pain cohort is dosed) | Full identity/QC on the new batch (loading, purity, size, release). Cytokine panel (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, IL-10) comparing GD-CBD, free CBD, and dendrimer alone, with a viability control on every plate. CB<sub>1</sub>/CB<sub>2</sub> binding. Targeted analytical check for an oxidized CBD byproduct (HU-331), a plausible but still unconfirmed explanation for the batch inconsistency above. |
| **Final pain efficacy cohort** | 9 rats: GD-CBD (n=4), vehicle (n=3), free CBD at the same dose (n=3). Blinded, randomized, matched to the original protocol (supplier, sex, age, dosing, behavioral method). Primary analysis pre-specified and fixed before unblinding. A pre-specified bar distinguishes "replicated" from "consistent" from "failed to replicate." |
| **Inflammatory marker sub-study** | Satellite group of 6 additional rats, sampled at 72h, near the peak of the behavioral effect. Main-cohort tissue is collected at day 7, likely too late to see the same signal. |
| **Biodistribution** (conditional) | Small imaging study, 5 rats, tracking a fluorescently labeled conjugate. Runs only if the labeled material passes a predefined QC checklist (purity, particle behavior, biological activity comparable to unlabeled drug). If QC fails, the study is dropped, not run on compromised material. The paper does not depend on it. |

# Literature and IP context

A literature check (2026-10-09) to keep the manuscript's claims accurate against the published record:

- **Free CBD already has published efficacy in incision pain models from other groups,** at a similar dose. The published dose-response is inconsistent across studies (effective in one, not in another; the effective dose differs by sex and hormonal state in females). **Manuscript claim:** GD-CBD's advantage over free CBD under matched conditions, not that free CBD has no effect.
- **No peer-reviewed paper exists yet from any group** on a dendrimer-CBD conjugate in a pain model. This would be a first report. Two things to address in the Discussion: this group's own 2025 conference abstract already describes the program publicly (pain and epilepsy), and a 2025 paper from another group reports a different, non-dendrimer CBD delivery approach also working in neuropathic pain.
- **A Johns Hopkins patent application covering this technology is confirmed pending** (filed 2023; broad scope; names CBD, the glucose and hydroxyl dendrimer platforms, and pain as a covered use). Favorable IP position. **Action:** confirm publication timing with Hopkins tech transfer or patent counsel before submission, since the application is pending, not issued.

# Main scientific limitations, stated plainly

| Limitation | How it's handled |
|:---|:---|
| Small sample sizes throughout (new pain cohort is 9 rats total). | Reported as effect sizes with confidence intervals, not a single definitive p-value. |
| Batch-to-batch inconsistency in the pain data, not fully explained. | Reported transparently, not minimized. |
| No carrier-only (dendrimer without CBD) control group in live animals. | Comparison made in vitro instead; limitation stated explicitly. |
| Mechanism not established. | Paper reports an inflammatory marker association, not a proven mechanism, in either indication. |
| Only female rats used in the pain cohort (matching the original positive cohorts). | Stated as a limitation; published estrous-phase sensitivity of CBD dosing noted as a likely contributing factor. |
| Biodistribution, if it runs, supports only a modest claim. | Signal higher at the injury site than the uninjured side in the same animal — not neuron-specific or receptor-specific targeting. |
