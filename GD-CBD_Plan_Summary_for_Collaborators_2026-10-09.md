# Purpose of this document

A summary of the plan to complete and publish the GD-CBD (glucose dendrimer-cannabidiol) postoperative pain work, for review by collaborators and PIs. It covers the strategy decision between a pain-only paper and a combined pain-plus-epilepsy paper, the remaining experiments, the timeline, and the main scientific risks. A sign-off on the combined-paper option is the main ask (see "What we need," below).

# Strategy: two paths, one decision point

**Default plan: a combined paper**, covering both the epilepsy data and the postoperative pain data, built around a shared finding: GD-CBD reduces inflammatory markers in both models, measured with the same cytokine panel (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, IL-10) in both species.

**Fallback: a pain-only short report**, with more conservative claims, if the combined paper is not approved by all PIs, or if the new pain cohort (below) does not replicate the earlier result.

| If the new pain cohort... | Then the paper is... |
|:---|:---|
| Replicates the earlier effect, or is at least consistent with it | Combined pain-plus-epilepsy paper. Pain is one supporting figure. |
| Does not replicate | Epilepsy-led paper, with chemistry and in vitro data. No pain efficacy claim made. |
| (Either result, if the combined paper isn't approved) | Pain-only short report, with conservative claims. |

The decision point is **2026-11-13**, once the new pain cohort is complete and unblinded.

**What needs to happen for the combined option to go forward:**
- Sign-off from all PIs involved, by 2026-10-30.
- The epilepsy dataset locked (no further changes) by 2026-11-06.
- The cytokine panel and assay platform matched between the two indications, so the marker data in both figures are directly comparable.

# Where the pain data currently stand

Honest accounting, because this shapes what the paper can and cannot claim:

- **Two original cohorts** showed a clear, sustained effect of GD-CBD (3 mg/kg IP) over 5+ days, both made from the same original CBD source material.
- **Several cohorts made with different material or methods since then have not reproduced the effect**, including one made with an alternate CBD vendor and one made from a different vial of the same vendor's CBD. One later cohort at a much higher dose showed a partial effect.
- **Standard chemical characterization (purity, size, NMR) has not predicted which batches work in vivo.** Two of the batches that failed in vivo looked clean by every standard analytical measure. The best available quality check right now is a functional one: whether the material reduces TNF-α release in an inflammatory cell assay.
- **The new cohort now in preparation uses a new CBD source lot from the original supplier,** and is the critical test: if it reproduces the original effect, this becomes a genuine replication, not just two cohorts that happened to use the same early material.

This history is reported transparently in the manuscript (a supplement table lists every cohort and preparation, including the ones that didn't work), not glossed over. The scientific claim rests on what replicates, not on the full historical record.

# Remaining experimental work

**Chemistry and in vitro (must complete before the pain cohort is dosed):**
- Full identity and quality check on the new batch: loading, purity, particle size, drug release.
- A cytokine panel (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, IL-10) comparing GD-CBD, free CBD, and the dendrimer alone, with a cell-viability control on every plate.
- CB<sub>1</sub>/CB<sub>2</sub> receptor binding, to characterize what the conjugate retains relative to free CBD.
- A targeted analytical check for an oxidized CBD byproduct (CBD hydroxyquinone, "HU-331") in the starting material and through the synthesis, since this is a plausible but still unconfirmed explanation for the batch-to-batch inconsistency above.

**Final pain efficacy cohort:**
- 9 rats: GD-CBD (n=4), vehicle (n=3), free CBD at the same dose (n=3).
- Blinded and randomized, matching the original protocol (same supplier, sex, age, dosing, behavioral method).
- A pre-specified primary analysis, fixed before the data are unblinded, so the replication result is not adjusted after the fact.
- A pre-specified bar for calling the result a replication versus merely "consistent" versus a failure to replicate (see the full technical plan for the statistical detail).

**Inflammatory marker sub-study:**
- A satellite group of 6 additional rats, sampled at 72 hours (near the peak of the behavioral effect) for inflammatory marker measurement, since the main cohort's tissue is collected at day 7, likely too late to see the same signal.

**Biodistribution (conditional, not guaranteed to run):**
- A small imaging study (5 rats) tracking a fluorescently labeled version of the conjugate, run only if the labeled material passes a predefined quality checklist (comparable purity, particle behavior, and biological activity to the unlabeled drug).
- If the labeled material does not pass that checklist, this study is dropped entirely rather than run on compromised material. The paper does not depend on it.

# Literature and intellectual property context

A literature check (2026-10-09) to make sure the manuscript's claims are accurate relative to the published record:

- **Free CBD itself already has published efficacy in incision pain models from other groups,** at a similar dose, though the published dose-response is inconsistent (an effective dose in one study is ineffective in another, and the effective dose differs by sex and hormonal state in female animals). The manuscript's claim is therefore about GD-CBD's advantage over free CBD in matched conditions, not that free CBD has no effect at all.
- **No peer-reviewed paper exists yet from any group on a dendrimer-CBD conjugate in a pain model,** so this would be a first report in the peer-reviewed literature. A conference abstract from this group already describes the program publicly (pain and epilepsy), and a 2025 paper from another group reports a different (non-dendrimer) CBD delivery approach also working in a neuropathic pain model; both are addressed in the manuscript's discussion of related work.
- **A Johns Hopkins patent application covering this technology is confirmed pending** (filed 2023, broad in scope, names CBD, the glucose and hydroxyl dendrimer platforms, and pain as a covered use). This is a favorable IP position, but because the application is pending rather than issued, **publication timing should be confirmed with Hopkins tech transfer or patent counsel before the manuscript is submitted.**

# Main scientific limitations, stated plainly

- **Small sample sizes throughout** (the new pain cohort is 9 rats total). Results are reported as effect sizes with confidence intervals, not as a single definitive p-value.
- **Batch-to-batch inconsistency in the pain data,** not fully explained. Reported transparently, not minimized.
- **No carrier-only (dendrimer without CBD) control group in the live animals.** This comparison is made in vitro instead; the limitation is stated explicitly in the paper.
- **Mechanism is not established.** The paper reports an inflammatory marker association, not a proven mechanism, in either indication.
- **Only female rats are used in the pain cohort** (matching the original positive cohorts); this is stated as a limitation, with the published estrous-phase sensitivity of CBD dosing noted as a likely contributing factor.
- **Biodistribution, if it runs at all, supports only a modest claim** (signal is higher at the injury site than the uninjured side in the same animal), not a claim of neuron-specific or receptor-specific targeting.
