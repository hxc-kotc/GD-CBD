**Abstract**

Acute postoperative pain remains inadequately resolved in a substantial proportion of surgical patients despite contemporary multimodal analgesic strategies. Opioid-related adverse effects and the risk of persistent postoperative opioid use necessitate effective non-opioid alternatives. Cannabidiol (CBD) modulates multiple molecular targets involved in nociceptive and inflammatory signaling, suggesting potential analgesic utility; however, its clinical translation has been limited by poor bioavailability and non-selective tissue distribution. Overcoming these pharmacokinetic constraints requires a delivery strategy that concentrates CBD within pain-relevant cell populations. Previous studies have shown that glucose dendrimers are preferentially internalized by hyperexcitable neurons and activated microglia, cells which express cannabinoid receptors and contribute critically to pain processing. Leveraging this selectivity, we conjugated CBD to a glucose dendrimer (GD-CBD) and evaluated its antinociceptive efficacy in a rat model of postoperative pain. When administered intraperitoneally, GD-CBD significantly attenuated incision-evoked mechanical hypersensitivity compared to equivalent doses of unconjugated CBD. These findings establish glucose dendrimer conjugation as a viable strategy for augmenting the analgesic efficacy of CBD through targeted delivery and driving forward the development of non-opioid therapeutics for postoperative pain.

**Keywords: cannabidiol, glucose dendrimer, postoperative pain, nanoparticle drug delivery**

# **1\. Introduction**

## **1.1 The Clinical Burden of Postoperative Pain**

Acute postoperative pain remains a pervasive challenge in contemporary surgical care. In a national survey of 250 postoperative adults in the United States, approximately 80% reported pain following surgery, with most indicating pain of moderate to extreme intensity (Apfelbaum, 2003). Although postoperative analgesic practices have evolved, recent evidence indicates that clinically significant pain frequently persists after discharge. A systematic review and meta-analysis found that the pooled prevalence of moderate-to-severe postoperative pain remained substantial across early recovery, including estimates of 31% of patients on the first day after discharge and 58% at one to two weeks after discharge, with considerable variation across studies, procedures, and assessment methods (Park, 2023). Inadequately managed postoperative pain is associated with delayed functional recovery, diminished quality of life, and, in a subset of patients, transition to chronic postsurgical pain (Gan, 2017; Snell, 2025). 

Current clinical guidelines recommend individualized, multimodal postoperative analgesia that integrates non-opioid pharmacotherapy, regional or local anesthetic techniques, and nonpharmacologic interventions (Chou, 2016). Opioids remain an important treatment option for moderate-to-severe acute postoperative pain; however, their perioperative utility is limited by dose-dependent adverse effects including nausea, constipation, and respiratory depression. Postoperative opioid exposure has also been associated with persistent opioid use in a subset of previously opioid-naïve patients, although incidence estimates vary substantially according to surgical procedure, patient population, follow-up duration, and study design (Jivraj,2020). These limitations necessitate effective opioid-sparing and non-opioid analgesic strategies that provide adequate postoperative pain relief. 

## **1.2 Cannabidiol: Mechanistic Rationale and Translational Barriers**

Cannabidiol (CBD) is a non-intoxicating phytocannabinoid derived from *Cannabis sativa* that has attracted considerable interest as a potential analgesic and anti-inflammatory agent. Unlike $∆$9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC), CBD does not produce its effects primarily through CB1 cannabinoid receptor agonism and does not cause the characteristic intoxication associated with THC (Vučković, 2018). Consistent with this pharmacology, CBD appears to have low abuse potential; however, clinically relevant central nervous system effects, including somnolence and fatigue, can occur, particularly at higher doses or in combination with other medications. CBD has therefore been investigated as a potential nonopioid analgesic candidate with a mechanistic profile distinct from both opioids and THC-containing cannabinoid products. 

CBD modulated multiple molecular targets implicated in nociceptive and inflammatory signaling. These include transient receptor potential vanilloid type 1 (TRPV1) channels, which play a central role in peripheral nociceptor activation, inflammatory sensitization, and thermal hyperalgesia (Louis-Gray, 2022). In preclinical and in vitro systems, CBD can activate TRPV1 at higher concentrations, followed by channel desensitization, a mechanism that may contribute to antinociceptive effects. CBD has also been reported to modulate serotonin 5-HT1A receptor signaling, which is relevant to descending pain modulation and anxiolysis. Additional proposed mechanisms include potentiation of glycine receptor function, inhibition of nucleoside transporters, and modulation of voltage-gated sodium channels implicated in neuronal excitability and neuropathic pain. Beyond these neuronal targets, CBD may exert anti-inflammatory effects by reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine release from activated immune cells and modulating signaling pathways including NF-KB signaling.

These multimodal mechanisms are biologically pertinent to postoperative pain, which involves tissue injury, inflammatory mediator release, peripheral nociceptor sensitization, and central amplification of pain signaling. Preclinical studies have demonstrated antinociceptive effects of CBD in diverse pain models, including inflammatory, neuropathic cancer-related, and incisional models. However, translation of these findings has been limited by the unfavorable physicochemical and pharmacokinetic properties of CBD. 

Human pharmacokinetic studies have demonstrated variable systemic exposure following oral administration Miller,2018. Food-dependent absorption is substantial; in adults with refractory epilepsy, coadministration with a high-fat meal significantly increased CBD exposure compared with fasting conditions Birnbaum,2019. In approved oral formulations for seizure disorders, chronic high-dose CBD is associated with somnolence, decreased appetite, diarrhea, and fatigue Epidiolex prescribing info,2024. Collectively, these properties impede reliable dose selection and complicate exposure-response interpretation, particularly in acute clinical settings requiring predictable tissue concentrations.

## **1.3 Glucose Dendrimer-Based Drug Delivery**

Dendrimers are synthetic, nanoscale macromolecules characterized by defined architecture, multivalent surface functionality, and tunable physicochemical properties. These features enable dendrimers to be engineered for drug conjugation, solubilization, and controlled release Wang,2022. In pain-relevant preclinical research, dendrimer-conjugated triamcinolone acetonide reduced spinal microglial activation and attenuated mechanical allodynia following peripheral nerve injury, demonstrating that dendrimer-based delivery can meaningfully alter drug distribution and pharmacodynamic effects in neuroinflammatory pain models Kim,2017. 

Glucose dendrimers represent a promising platform for neural drug delivery. Unlike conventional polyamidoamine (PAMAM) dendrimers, glucose dendrimers feature surface glucose moieties that may facilitate cellular uptake through glucose transporter-mediated mechanisms. Recent preclinical work reported selective uptake of glucose dendrimer by hyperexcitable neurons in a rodent epilepsy model, supporting their investigation as biologically targeted delivery vehicles in neurological contexts characterized by altered cell metabolism or activity (Sharma, 2024). This selective targeting property provides rationale for evaluating glucose dendrimer-based delivery in postoperative pain, where peripheral nociceptor sensitization and central neuronal hyperexcitability contribute to pain-related behaviors.

## **1.4 Study Rationale and Objectives**

In the present study, we developed a glucose dendrimer–cannabidiol conjugate (GD-CBD) and evaluated its efficacy in the rat plantar incision model, a well-characterized preclinical model of post incisional pain (Brennan, 1996). This model produces quantifiable mechanical hypersensitivity and guarding behavior following a standardized plantar hindpaw incision, permitting assessment of treatment effects on evoked pain-related endpoints.

We hypothesized that a single postoperative dose of GD-CBD would significantly attenuate incision-evoked mechanical hypersensitivity compared with vehicle treatment. Additionally, we compared GD-CBD with free CBD to determine whether dendrimer conjugation confers pharmacodynamic advantages. Paw thickness was measured as a secondary endpoint to assess treatment effects on postoperative edema.

**2.2 Animal model of**   
Adult female Sprague-Dawley rats (8–10 weeks of age, 250–300 g body weight) were obtained from Inotiv. Animals were housed in groups of 2–3 per cage under controlled environmental conditions (temperature: 21 ± 0.5 °C; relative humidity: 55% ± 5%; 12-hour light/dark cycle). Standard rodent chow and water were provided ad libitum. 

All experimental procedures were approved by the Johns Hopkins University Animal Care and Use Committee (\#RA24M356, approved February 28, 2025\)

**2.3 Flank Incision Model**  
Animals underwent a structured 10-day habituation protocol including acclimation to the testing environment, apparatus, and von Frey stimulation procedure. Baseline mechanical withdrawal thresholds were established two consecutive days prior to surgery. 

Under isoflurane anesthesia, a 2cm longitudinal skin incision was made on the left flank and closed with interrupted 3-0 nylon sutures. Immediately post-surgery, animals received a single intraperitoneal injection of vehicle (saline with 5% acetonitrile), free CBD (3, 10, or 30 mg/kg in vehicle), or GD-CBD (3 mg/kg CBD equivalent in vehicle). Treatment assignment was randomized, and investigators performing behavioral assessments were blinded to group allocation. The study was conducted in three independent cohorts. 

**2.4 Behavioral Assessment**  
Mechanical withdrawal thresholds were assessed using calibrated von Frey filaments and the up-down method at baseline and daily for 7 days post-surgery. Testing sites were located 3–4 mm from the incision on the dorsal and ventral aspects. A positive response was defined as brisk withdrawal, flinching, or vocalization. The 50% withdrawal was calculated using the Dixon formula. On day 7, a subset of animals received perilesional ropivacaine (0.25%, 200uL) to validate that hypersensitivity reflected ongoing peripheral nociceptive input

**2.5 Biodistribution Studies**  
Separate cohorts received D-Cy5 immediately following flank incision or in uninjured animals. Dendrimer accumulation was quantified by fluorescence measurement of tissue homogenates and ex vivo IVIS imaging. 

**2.6 Histological Assessment**  
At study termination (Day 7), incision sites were excised, fixed in 10% formalin, paraffin-embedded, section, and stained.
