**Rationale:** NMDA receptors help drive cortical spreading depression and central sensitization in migraine/trigeminal pathways. Blocking NMDA signaling can abort/reverse these processes.   
Compared with free ketamine, a glucose-dendrimer conjugate may (i) bias delivery to hyperactive neurons implicated in migraine circuitry and enable lower total dose for the same central effect, enabling use for ED/inpatient rescue and outpatient use.

**Indications:**

1. **Status migrainosus (IV; inpatient/ED rescue).** Best mechanistic/clinical fit; existing ketamine practice provides a comparator framework. Could be the best expansion from GD-Ket for SE.  
2. **Refractory chronic migraine.** Evidence suggests short-term benefit in highly refractory patients; GD-Ket could improve tolerability.   
3. **Migraine with prolonged aura.** Small but positive RCT signal for ketamine on aura severity; GD-Ket may replicate with fewer side effects (route to consider later: intranasal or oral-rapid).   
4. **Cluster** **headaches.** Glutamatergic/NMDA-receptor-mediated sensitization in trigeminal pathways. Blocking NMDA can interrupt central sensitization and trigeminal nociceptive transmission.

**Models**  
**1\. Acute Nitroglycerin (NTG) model (can also do repeated model over weeks)**

* Design: NTG (10 mg/kg, IP). Treat therapeutically at peak allodynia (e.g., 60-90 min post-NTG).  
* Arms (N=12/group): Vehicle, GD-Ket (Low/Mid/High, IV), free ketamine, optional sumatriptan (benchmark, small N).  
* Endpoints: Periorbital von Frey (primary), light/dark box (photophobia), open-field & rotarod (sedation/ataxia control).  
* Efficacy: ≥30% increase in periorbital threshold vs vehicle and non-inferior to ketamine 

**2\. CGRP-provoked photophobia/allodynia** 

* Design: hα-CGRP (systemic). Dose GD-Ket therapeutically (post-CGRP) ± a small pre-treat cohort   
* Arms (N=12/group): Vehicle, GD-Ket (2 doses), free ketamine.  
* Endpoints: Light time (primary), periorbital von Frey (secondary), c-Fos (TNC) in a subset (n=6).  
* Efficacy: ≥25% increase time-in-light vs vehicle and meaningful c-Fos reduction vs vehicle.

**GD-cannabidiol for chronic neuropathic pain**  
**1\. Scientific & Clinical Rationale**

* **Need**   
* Many patients with diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN), chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN), post-herpetic neuralgia (PHN), and radiculopathies remain inadequately controlled on gabapentinoids/SNRIs/TCAs where tolerability and toxicity is limiting.  
* **Opportunity**  
* CBD engages TRPV1, 5-HT1A, voltage-gated sodium channels, and neuro-immune modulation; clinically validated safety from Epidiolex.  
* Glucose-dendrimer targeting is expected to concentrate exposure in injured neurons and activated microglia/macrophages (DRG, dorsal horn, injured nerve) providing stronger efficacy at lower systemic exposure, fewer adverse events, and no drug-drug interactions  
* Oral enables chronic outpatient use.

**Target product profile:** Oral capsule/tablet; once-daily; ≥30%-50% pain responder rate; improved sleep/function; opioid-sparing as supportive claim; liver-safe with routine monitoring.

**2\. IND-enabling preclinical research plan**  
**a. Formulation & PK/PD (Oral)**

* **Formulation options:** tablet of spray-dried dispersion; consider enteric coating; ensure content uniformity at low mg strengths for flexible titration.  
* **Oral PK:** rat and large animal; bioavailability, food effect, brain/DRG/nerve distribution; free-CBD impurity profile; conjugation % stability in GI conditions.  
* **Tissue targeting:** Labeled GD-CBD to map uptake in injured nerve, DRG, spinal cord, vs healthy tissues  
  * POC and additional characterization under way in post-operative pain model

**b. Efficacy Models**  
Run at least two mechanistic classes \+ one metabolic:

* **Peripheral nerve injury:** **Sciatic Nerve Ligation**/**Chronic Constriction Injury (CCI)**: mechanical allodynia (von Frey), thermal hyperalgesia (Hargreaves), cold allodynia (acetone)  
  * Model development under way  
* **Chemotherapy neuropathy:** **Paclitaxel** or oxaliplatin CIPN: mechanical/cold allodynia, movement  
  * Model development planned  
* **Sarcoma model**  
* **Diabetic neuropathy:** STZ-induced mechanical/thermal thresholds; optional corneal confocal microscopy for small-fiber density   
* **Mechanistic readouts:** c-Fos (dorsal horn), Iba1/GFAP (microglia/astrocytes), cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6), pERK, Nav channel modulation ex vivo; DRG/dorsal horn electrophysiology.

**c. Safety / DDI / Abuse Liability**

* Oral repeat-dose tox (28-90 days) in two species to cover chronic use; focus on LFTs (CBD class effect), GI, neurobehavior.  
* DDI panels geared to pain SOC: duloxetine, pregabalin, TCAs, tramadol/opioids (PK and tolerability).  
* Abuse liability: preclinical CPP/locomotor; HAP may not be required but plan for FDA discussion.

**d. CMC (shared where possible)**

* Same dendrimer backbone controls (MW distribution, residual solvents, endotoxin).  
* Release spec for % conjugation and free CBD (\<X%); ICH stability (25/60 & 40/75); photostability.  
* Linker strategy: primary plan \= non-enzymatic slow-release or activity-while-bound; keep a backup esterase-cleavable linker if PD requires partial release.
