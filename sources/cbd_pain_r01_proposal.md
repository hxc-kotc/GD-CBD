# Cell-Targeted Dendrimer-Cannabidiol (CBD) Conjugates for Acute and Chronic Pain (R01 Proposal)

---

## A. SIGNIFICANCE

There is an urgent need for safe, effective, and non-addictive analgesia for both acute and chronic pain. Pain is among the most common reasons for medical care, with about **21% of U.S. adults reporting chronic pain** and about **7% reporting high-impact chronic pain** that limits daily activities. Acute post-operative pain remains inadequately controlled, with **49% of patients reporting moderate-to-severe pain** on post-operative day 1, which is linked to delayed recovery and persistent pain. 

Chronic pain is resistant to most medical treatment, leading to long-term disorders with psychological effects and a significant reduction in quality of life—including loss of the ability to eat, sleep, work, or function normally—alongside irritability, decreased energy levels, weight changes, depression, and an increased risk of suicide. 

The public-health impact of opioids remains profound, with **105,000 U.S. overdose deaths in 2023**, most involving opioids. Opioids provide short-lived relief, contribute to tolerance and dependence, and are associated with opioid-induced hyperalgesia and adverse events. Regional analgesia is time-limited, often complicated by rebound pain and toxicity risks. For neuropathic pain, drugs such as tricyclics, SNRIs, and gabapentinoids have modest average efficacy and frequent side effects, leading to high discontinuation rates. These gaps highlight the need for new, durable, non-addictive pain therapeutics that directly engage immune and neuronal drivers of pain.

### Cannabidiol (CBD) and its Limitations
Cannabidiol (CBD) has strong mechanistic rationale in acute and chronic pain:
* **Neuronal Level:** Activates and then desensitizes TRPV1/TRPA1, reduces excitability via state-dependent block of $Na_v1.7$ and $Na_v1.8$, modulates $5-HT_1A$ signaling, and potentiates inhibitory glycine receptor currents in the dorsal horn.
* **Immune & Glial Level:** Shifts macrophage and microglial programs toward anti-inflammatory states through $CB_2$-related signaling, $PPAR\gamma$ activation, and ENT1 inhibition to counter peripheral and central sensitization.

**Limitations of Free CBD:**
* Poor aqueous solubility and low oral bioavailability with strong first-pass metabolism and food effects.
* Short systemic half-life and broad tissue distribution necessitating higher or more frequent dosing.
* Clinically relevant drug-drug interactions (DDIs) from inhibition of major metabolic enzymes and transporters.
* Epidiolex is associated with somnolence, diarrhea, decreased appetite, DDIs, and an antiepileptic-class warning for suicidal ideation.
* Much of the CBD dose reaches off-target tissues rather than pain-driving neurons and immune cells.

### Dendrimer-Based Nanomedicine Platform
Dendrimers are tree-like polymeric nanoparticles with unique physicochemical properties (high density of surface functional groups) that can be tailored for cell-specific targeting. 
* **Hydroxyl-Terminated Polyamidoamine Dendrimers (HD):** Selectively localize in activated microglia and deliver drugs to the site of injury with minimal uptake in healthy tissues. HDs are nontoxic (even at $\$500+\text{mg/kg}$) and cleared intact through the kidney.
* **Clinical Validation:** 
  * *HD-NAC (OP-101®):* Significantly improved outcomes in preclinical models and hospitalized severe COVID-19 patients in a Phase 2a trial (reduced mortality by 40%, decreased brain injury biomarkers).
  * *Dendrimer-Sunitinib Analog:* Completed a Phase 2a trial for wet AMD and DME demonstrating bilateral retinal disease treatment without adverse events.
* **Glucose-Based Dendrimers (GD):** Designed to enhance glucose transporter interactions for **neuron targeting** while maintaining the hydroxyl density ($\>1\text{ OH/nm}^2$) and unique physicochemical properties of HD ($4\text{ nm}, 14\text{ kDa}$). GD leverages natural glucose metabolism via multivalent surface interactions with glucose transporters (GLUT/SGLUT) to deliver payloads to metabolically stressed, hyperexcitable neurons without being metabolized itself.

### Dual Cell Targeting Strategy
* **HD-CBD:** Targets activated microglia and macrophages to modulate neuroimmune programs sustaining hypersensitivity.
* **GD-CBD:** Targets metabolically stressed neurons to directly dampen nociceptor excitability.

## B. INNOVATION

* **Engineering Dendrimer Composition:** Tailoring hydroxyl density and glucose moieties to target key cells in pain (reactive macrophages/microglia, injured neurons in the DRG) from systemic administration.
* **Dual Cell Targeting for Analgesia:** HD targets reactive microglia/macrophages; GD expands targeting to metabolically stressed neurons.
* **Comparative Evaluation:** Assessing HD and GD conjugate localization and efficacy across acute plantar incision (PI) and chronic constriction injury (CCI) models.
* **Improved Pharmacokinetics:** Converting CBD into a highly water-soluble, renally-cleared conjugate with controlled release, reduced off-target exposure, and reduced sedation, while preserving $CB_2$, $PPAR\gamma$, adenosine, TRP, and $Na_v$ activity.
* **Public Health Alignment:** Delivering durable, non-addictive analgesia with dose-sparing compared to free CBD to significantly reduce peri-operative and chronic opioid use.

## C. APPROACH

### C.1 Preliminary Results
1. **HD Nanoparticles:** Nontoxic, cleared intact via kidneys, and selectively target reactive microglia/macrophages associated with neuroinflammation in over 30 animal models (including primates). Confined to reactive macrophages at sciatic nerve chronic constriction injury (CCI) sites following IP administration.
2. **Glucose Dendrimers (GD):** $4.4\text{ nm}$, neutrally charged, 24 surface glucose molecules, $>99\%$ purity. Cleared intact via kidneys without hepatic degradation. Targets injured neurons and microglia experiencing metabolic stress and upregulated glucose transporters.
3. **GD Target Peripheral Pain Pathway (CCI):** Rapidly and persistently accumulates along the sensory pathway (sciatic injury site, DRG neuronal bodies, dorsal root, and spinal cord dorsal horn) within minutes and retains signal for $\ge 10$ days.
4. **Complementary Localization (Flank Incision Model):** HD-Cy5 preferentially labels $Iba1$-positive macrophages, whereas GD-Cy5 labels both $Iba1$-positive macrophages and $TUJ1$-stained neurons in the DRG and wound tissue after a single systemic dose.
5. **Synthesis of GD-CBD:** $CBD-\text{PEG}_4\text{-azide}$ conjugated to GD via $\text{Cu(I)}$-catalyzed click chemistry ($\text{CuAAC}$). Results in a neutrally charged conjugate ($6.04 \pm 0.8\text{ nm}$, $12.5\%\text{ w/w}$ CBD loading, $\sim 7$ CBD molecules per dendrimer, $17.6\text{ kDa}$ MW). Retains comparable $CB_2$ $\text{EC}_{50}$ activity ($7.8\ \mu\text{M}$) and increases aqueous solubility to $>200\text{ mg/mL}$ (vs. $0.7\text{-}10\ \mu\text{g/mL}$ for free CBD).
6. **In Vivo Analgesia (Rat Flank Incision):** A single $3\text{ mg/kg}$ IP dose of GD-CBD provides rapid onset (within 2 hours) and sustained reversal of mechanical hypersensitivity for **5 days**, outperforming free CBD and vehicle controls without sedation or behavioral changes.
7. **Microsomal Stability (GD-Ketamine):** GD-ketamine showed **no detectable turnover** in human liver microsomes over 4 hours, unlike free ketamine and norketamine, indicating shielding from hepatic enzymes and reduced DDI risk.

### C.2 Research Design & Specific Aims

* **Aim 1: Synthesize and characterize HD and GD conjugates of CBD.**
  * Synthesize releasable HD-CBD and GD-CBD via click chemistry with $\sim 10\%$ drug loading.
  * Characterize via NMR, MALDI-TOF, HPLC, DLS, and zeta potential.
  * *Milestone:* Reproducibly synthesize D-CBD conjugates with $>98\%$ purity, $>200\text{ mg/mL}$ solubility, and controlled release over 72 hours.
* **Aim 2: Define in vitro cellular uptake, biocompatibility, metabolism, and DDI liabilities.**
  * Profile binding and signaling across $CB_1$, $CB_2$, $\text{GPR55}$, $PPAR\gamma$, $\text{ENT1}$, $\text{TRPV1}$, $\text{TRPA1}$, and $5-\text{HT}_1\text{A}$.
  * Evaluate cellular uptake in primary DRG neurons, HMC3 microglial cells, and THP-1 macrophages.
  * Assess anti-inflammatory profiles under LPS and inflammatory cocktail/paclitaxel injury mimic conditions.
  * Test human liver microsomal clearance and CYP/UGT inhibition panels.
* **Aim 3: Establish in vivo localization, efficacy, and safety in acute and chronic pain models.**
  * *Task 3.1 (Localization):* Quantify fluorescent HD-CBD and GD-CBD localization in DRG, sciatic nerve, spinal cord, and peri-incisional tissues in Plantar Incision (PI) and CCI models.
  * *Task 3.2 (Efficacy):* Evaluate dose-response in acute PI model ($3, 15, 45\text{ mg/kg}$ IP vs. free CBD, vehicle, and ketorolac) and chronic efficacy in CCI model (dosed every 3 days over 28 days vs. gabapentin).
  * *Task 3.3 (Safety/Toxicity):* Single-dose acute toxicity study at $250\text{ mg/kg}$ equivalent IP assessing clinical signs, clinical chemistry (ALT, AST, etc.), hematology, and histopathology at 24 hours.
