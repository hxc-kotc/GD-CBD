## 2. Paper 1: GD-CBD (combined epilepsy and pain)

### 2.1 Status and framing (Spencer)
- GD-CBD is a long-running project, "getting really close to the end." A new GD-CBD batch looks very good and is validated in vitro. It was made with the original material from the original batch ("CRM material from caiman" in the transcript — slide confirms: "Cayman CRM material (same as first batch)"). High hopes for in vivo.

- Spencer: it "all hangs on whether this next in vivo cohort works or not."
- **Proposed narrative:** CBD is used off label across many indications but is not widely adopted because of (1) formulation: not soluble in water or saline, so it has to be in oil, which limits it to transdermal or mostly oral use; (2) off-target effects, mainly in chronic use: sedation and hepatotoxicity.
- **What the dendrimer fixes:** glucose dendrimer makes CBD much more soluble and improves the therapeutic index (lower dose needed). Bonus seen in both models tested: durability, a single dose works for multiple days.
- **Market angle:** the only FDA-approved CBD is Epidiolex, approved for about 3 smaller indications. This would give access to many more.
- Aside on dose: B said the dose is very high ("100 mg") and that it is for children. Kannan said people think 100 mg is not high for CBD and eat it in gram quantities. Context for the 100 mg is unclear in the audio.

### 2.4 Safety arm: none planned (as of the discussion)
- Spencer asked whether a safety arm is needed. Answer from Sujatha and Spencer: there is nothing on safety right now.
- Kannan: cannot show much because CBD itself is safe. Sujatha: the only real way to show CBD safety is prolonged reproductive or developing-brain toxicity work.
- Spencer cited one high-dose toxicity paper at about 700 mg/kg (hepatotoxicity and behavioral effects, oral). Sujatha: may be formulation related.
- Kannan on why a dose-matching tox study is not feasible: at 700 mg/kg CBD you would need the conjugate at roughly ten times that (about 7 g/kg), which is impractical. His arithmetic in the audio is garbled. Sujatha added a concern about RBCs and blood volume at such doses. Spencer: it is oral, "that's not how we would dose it."
- F asked whether it is worth adding clinical chemistry on baseline blood inside the efficacy model. Sujatha: nothing will show up, and CBD vs dendrimer CBD would not be different. Kannan: saying anything gives reviewers something to poke. Sujatha's option: 10x the dose in a few animals and send blood for liver and kidney. Kannan and Sujatha: that would use up the good conjugate. Spencer agreed. **Outcome: not pursued** (no explicit vote; the objections were cost and reviewer exposure). Sujatha also said dendrimer non-toxicity is already shown in many studies at much higher doses.
- **How to handle safety in the paper:** Kannan: conjugates have typically been safe, so there is a general safety argument from GD. Cite the GD-Ket paper, say GD gives a much higher therapeutic index so CBD's negative effects can be diminished. This goes in the Discussion, not a figure.
- Also defensible, per Kannan: conjugating CBD and formulating it is a big deal, and there are few CBD polymer conjugates.
- Later, Spencer skipped a slide on an extremely high dose ("we're not going to address here"). Kannan joked that Bill Gates could not afford it and Elon Musk could, which reinforces the cost objection.
- **Note:** despite this "not pursued" discussion of a *dose-matched conjugate* tox study, the slide deck's own Figure 6 proposes a *separate, lower-cost* design (free CBD at literature doses, oral, in healthy animals — not spending scarce conjugate) and asks "Worthwhile?" as an open question.
