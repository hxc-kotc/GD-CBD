## 3. Paper 2: noribogaine and ibogaine

### 3.1 Spencer's framing and data so far
- Ibogaine is a root extract used indigenously, now being developed for opioid use disorder and other substance use disorders. Small studies show strong efficacy (hard to blind).
- **Main problem:** ibogaine is severely cardiotoxic, people die. If the dendrimer removes that, it becomes clinically usable.
- **Cardiac data now:** (1) zebrafish: not cardiotoxic, and zebrafish are a good model for the specific channel ibogaine blocks vs humans. (2) Cardiomyocytes, first cohort: no prolongation of field potential duration (the QT equivalent). Second cohort just finished. Spencer: with these two "we're probably 90%" of the way to the gold standard. The next level, primary human cardiomyocytes, is too costly for now.
- Early data on binding and neurite growth. An in vivo protocol is pending for proof of concept efficacy with dendrimer noribogaine.
- **Through end of year:** a few more receptors and channels, finish characterization (serum stability is a big one: confirm the drug is not released in blood where it could reach the heart), a couple more cohorts, at least a couple of opioid-withdrawal cohorts.
- **Next big efficacy step:** alcohol self-administration (an "AED" model per the transcript) in rats. Costs a lot of dendrimer.

### 3.3 What the first paper should be (Kannan, the core structural guidance)
- Many effective drugs act in multiple ways, and if the lab claims it will work out the mechanism it "will never convince anybody." The first papers should focus on mechanism at the level of: what is the difference between free drug and dendrimer drug, with a good handle that the conjugate keeps the free drug's activity within a factor of 2 to 3. Expand later.
- Do not try to delineate which sub-aspects of ibogaine action are involved. The biology groups "will drag you into a massive fight." Analogy: in 20 years of N-acetylcysteine work the lab says only "broad anti-inflammatory, broad antioxidant." "Ibogaine is God's compound," so people will drag the lab in.
- Plan: first do a "beautiful self-contained mechanistic binding study," then a very good publication. It would be big because of impact. With more resources later, delineate differences among free ibogaine, free noribogaine, and dendrimer noribogaine.
- First publication should also carry quick proof of concept efficacy. Opioid withdrawal is the application the lab would translate to and is a big priority for the project.
- **Which in vivo model:** C: do opioid withdrawal first, it is a large population, measures are easier, and it is quicker. Alcohol self-administration takes time to get animals to level, and Spencer noted it needs a lot of conjugate in rats. Kannan: "use mouse" (apparently to cut conjugate use; opioid withdrawal is already planned in mice, see 3.4). Spencer agreed.
- **Timing:** Kannan: this paper probably will not make the next 2 months (so it will not help with his count or "Durga's bet"), but early next year, and it could go in something really big.
- Spencer: there are 3 categories of data for this initial paper (the audio does not list them; the discussion covered cardiac safety and biodistribution, binding, and in vivo efficacy plus in vitro neuroplasticity).
- Spencer's next step: build a matrix of binding experiments, identify which are inexpensive, and find the minimum set of binding sites.
