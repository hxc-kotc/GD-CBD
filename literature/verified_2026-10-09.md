# Literature verification, 2026-10-09 (Perplexity, with citations)

**Provenance:** this supersedes the caveats in `search_notes_2026-10-09.md` (plain web search, titles/summaries only). This pass used Perplexity with full citations (DOIs/PMIDs given below) and is strong enough to cite from, though the underlying papers themselves have not been read in full by anyone on this side — spot-check key numbers before they go in a submitted manuscript.

## 1. Free CBD in incision pain: inverted-U, not a flat effective dose

All three papers used mechanical (von Frey) testing after a surgical incision, acute IP CBD, and found 3 mg/kg as a frequent but not universal effective point, with higher doses often weaker.

**A. Redmond et al. 2026**, *Pharmaceuticals (Basel)* 19(1):43, doi:10.3390/ph19010043, PMID 41599645. Dorsum incision, male Sprague-Dawley, n=9/group. CBD 3, 10, or 30 mg/kg IP, Cremophor:ethanol:saline 1:1:18, single dose on postop day 8, tested 60 min later. **3 mg/kg significantly raised ipsilateral-dorsum threshold** (Kruskal-Wallis χ²(4)=26.1, p<0.001; CBD3 vs vehicle p<0.01). 10 mg/kg p=0.063, 30 mg/kg p=0.136 — neither significant. No effect at contralateral dorsum or either hind paw (all p>0.05). Effect was site-restricted to primary hypersensitivity near the incision only.

**B. Arantes et al. 2024**, *Behavioural Brain Research* 459:114793, doi:10.1016/j.bbr.2023.114793, PMID 38048909. Plantar incision, male and female Wistar (females staged by vaginal cytology), CBD from BSPG-Pharm (99.6% pure), vehicle 2% Tween 80 + saline. Tested 24h post-incision, CBD dosed acutely, mechanical testing to 150 min.
- Males: 3 mg/kg effective (onset 30 min, persisted to 150 min); 0.3 mg/kg ineffective.
- Proestrus females: 3 mg/kg effective, onset delayed to 90 min; 0.3 mg/kg ineffective.
- Late-diestrus females: 0.3 mg/kg effective (onset 90 min); i.e., the effective dose flips with estrous stage.

**C. Genaro et al. 2017**, *Frontiers in Pharmacology* 8:391, doi:10.3389/fphar.2017.00391, PMID 28680401. Plantar incision, male Wistar only (n=275 total across experiments). Doses tested: 0.3, 1, 3, 10, 30 mg/kg IP. **3 mg/kg gave the clearest, longest mechanical effect** (to ≥150 min, peak at 60 min); 10 mg/kg significant only at the 60 min point; 0.3, 1, and 30 mg/kg not significant — a bell-shaped curve. Separately, CBD 1–3 mg/kg (including a dose, 1 mg/kg, that did NOT affect mechanical threshold) blocked conditioned place preference from nerve block, i.e., affected the affective/motivational pain dimension at a lower dose than the sensory one.

**Use in the manuscript:** frame GD-CBD's effect against this backdrop as dose-independence/duration advantage, not as "free CBD doesn't work." Free CBD's own literature dose-response is already nonmonotonic and state-dependent; a single 3 mg/kg GD-CBD dose giving a sustained 5-day effect is the comparison worth making, and the comparison is fair only if our free-CBD arm is dosed and timed consistently with what's shown to work elsewhere.

## 2. Patent: confirmed, pending, broad, explicitly covers CBD + pain

- **Title:** "Cannabinoid Dendrimer Compositions for Targeted Delivery."
- **Priority:** US provisional 63/401,464, filed 2022-08-26.
- **PCT:** PCT/US2023/072955, filed 2023-08-25.
- **Assignee:** The Johns Hopkins University.
- **Inventors:** Rangaramanujam Kannan, Sujatha Kannan, Kunal Parikh, Anjali Sharma, Wathsala Liyanage.
- **Status:** pending (not issued). National/regional phase entries identified as pending: EP23773125.2A, AU2023329445A, CN202380062107.6A, KR1020257010002A, CN119789874A (published). A US publication number (US 2026/0102502 A1) was identified in indexing but prosecution status wasn't independently confirmed.
- **Scope:** broad composition claim — a dendrimer conjugated to at least one natural or synthetic cannabinoid, covalent preferred. Explicitly includes glucose dendrimers (G1-G3), hydroxyl-PAMAM dendrimers (G3-G6), CBD by name, cleavable and non-cleavable linkers, ester/amide/disulfide/ether chemistry, and indications including acute/chronic/neuropathic pain, cancer pain, epilepsy, TBI, MS, neurodegeneration, inflammatory disease.
- **Does not** claim a specific dose regimen (uses "therapeutically effective amount" language), so it doesn't pre-empt or validate the 3 mg/kg efficacy finding itself.

**Action:** loop in Hopkins tech transfer / patent counsel on publication timing before submission. Pending status can interact with disclosure timing in some jurisdictions — this is worth a real conversation, not an assumption either way.

## 3. No peer-reviewed dendrimer-CBD pain competitor — but two adjacent public disclosures to account for

- **No peer-reviewed or preprint paper** found reporting a dendrimer- or nanoparticle-*conjugated* (covalent) CBD tested in any pain model, 2024-2026, from any group.
- **Already public, from this lab:** a 2025 Controlled Release Society conference abstract, "Glucose Dendrimer-Cannabidiol Conjugates: Safe and Effective Treatment of Pain and Epilepsy" — a meeting abstract, not peer-reviewed, but it already states the pain+epilepsy framing publicly. The manuscript's claims need to be consistent with what this abstract already said, not smaller and not bigger without new data to back it.
- **A real, if indirect, competitor:** Feng, Page, Chung, He, Wang, *Cell Chemical Biology*, 2025, doi:10.1016/j.chembiol.2025.10.005, PMID 41205612. A non-covalent CBD nano-micelle formulation ("CBD-IN") fully suppressed allodynia/hyperalgesia after a single dose in a mouse neuropathic pain model. Not a dendrimer conjugate, but it is a 2025, well-powered, nanoformulated-CBD-for-pain paper from another group, and should be cited and distinguished (delivery mechanism, model, species) in the Discussion.

## 4. Glucose dendrimer (GD2) biodistribution: confirmed, epilepsy-only, no pain/DRG data anywhere

Sharma et al. 2024, *Bioeng Transl Med* 9(5):e10655, doi:10.1002/btm2.10655, PMID 39553433. Confirmed: neuronal uptake shown in glutamate-excited primary cultures, ex vivo rabbit cerebral-palsy brain slices, and a mouse pilocarpine-seizure model; uptake reduced by blocking neuronal activity or glucose transporters (GLUT/SGLT mechanism implied). **No DRG, no peripheral nerve, no pain-model, no peripheral sensory neuron data anywhere in the literature** for this platform. The cannabinoid-dendrimer patent (above) *describes* DRG/PNS targeting as an intended application, but a patent's stated intent is not published biodistribution data and should never be cited as if it were.

## 5. HU-331: a usable QC method exists; no vendor-comparison data exists

- **No study found** directly comparing HU-331 content in Cayman vs. Dalton material, or plant-derived vs. synthetic CBD generally. Do not claim a vendor difference without lot-specific data.
- **Real evidence HU-331 forms post-synthesis, not just at it:** Thomson et al. 2023, *Scientific Reports* 13:6967, doi:10.1038/s41598-023-33815-7, PMID 37117252. Fresh CBD e-liquid: HU-331 0.05±0.04 µg/g; the raw CBD isolate it was made from already had 0.86±0.11 µg/g (oxidation pre-existing before formulation). After 10 weeks storage at ambient conditions: dark storage reached 116.03 µg/g HU-331; light/dark cycling reached 25.04 µg/g (and a visible color change to purple). HU-331 itself is photolabile and generates further reactive degradation products under light.
- **Usable QC method:** Kogan, Peters, Mechoulam, *Molecules* 2021, 26(6):1761, doi:10.3390/molecules26061761. HU-331 has characteristic UV maxima near 270 and ~409 nm (visible absorption — explains the purple color) and reacts with free thiols (e.g., NAC) to form covalent adducts — relevant since it could consume nucleophilic coupling reagents in conjugation chemistry, not just sit as an inert impurity.
- **Recommended protocol (stability-indicating):** HPLC-UV/DAD or LC-MS/MS for parent CBD, with a targeted HU-331 assay (reference standard, MRM transition, or UV at ~409 nm) run at every stage: starting CBD vial, activated intermediate, crude conjugation mixture, purified conjugate, and after storage. Source-fingerprint markers (CBDV, CBDB, CBDH, CBDP) can distinguish natural vs. synthetic CBD impurity profiles if source needs confirming (Brighenti et al. 2024, *Anal Bioanal Chem* 416(20):4555-4569, doi:10.1007/s00216-024-05396-5, PMID 38940871 — does not itself quantify HU-331).
